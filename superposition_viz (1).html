Toy Models of Superposition · interactive companion

Watching superposition form, break, and bite back

Three linked views: how feature embeddings fold themselves into simple polyhedra over the course of training, how that same packing trades away adversarial robustness, and what the resulting weight matrix looks like one neuron at a time. Every panel below is driven by the sliders above it — nothing here is a static picture.

01Geometric Transformations

Learning as a sequence of geometric transformations

Six features, arranged as two correlated triples, are compressed into three hidden dimensions. Rather than moving straight to its final arrangement, the network passes through distinct regimes — each one a simple geometric move. Drag the training-step slider to scrub through the trajectory; the loss panel on the right tracks the same moment, and each regime shows up as a distinct plateau-then-drop.

02Adversarial Robustness

Superposition creates a channel for adversarial attacks

Without superposition, an optimal L2 attack can only reach the one feature it targets. With superposition, the small interference terms in WᵀW give an attacker a way to reach a feature indirectly through its neighbors — and that channel widens as more features get packed into the same dimensions (higher sparsity). Adjust the attack budget to see how much the gap between the two conditions actually costs.

03Neuron-Level View

Reading superposition off a single weight matrix

Each column below is one hidden neuron; each stacked segment is one entry of that neuron's column in W, colored by the feature (row) it belongs to. Segment height is the magnitude of the weight, and negative weights are drawn below the axis. At low sparsity the model only affords to represent as many features as it has dimensions, cleanly, one per neuron. Push sparsity up and watch extra features get squeezed in, sharing neurons with small, mixed-sign interference terms.
